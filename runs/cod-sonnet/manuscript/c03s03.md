Lee came down the row with a printed sheet in each hand and stopped behind Sam's chair, which was where he stopped when he had already decided.

"Grace Lin," he said. "Emergency template six. Six thousand, twenty-four hours. Ana writes it because Ana knows her voice. Sam sends it because Sam's the day shift and the man expects the day shift."

"She's been in six months," Sam said. "He hasn't sent a cent."

"That's why it's an emergency." Lee set the sheet on the desk and squared it to the edge. Its title was EMERGENCY-6K-A, and under the title were fields in brackets: [FACILITY], [CONDITION], [AMOUNT], [WINDOW]. Someone had already filled in [AMOUNT] and [WINDOW] in pen. Nobody had filled in the rest.

Ana took it without looking at Lee. Her left hand stayed under the desk. With the right she wrote for eleven minutes, and twice she crossed out a line and wrote it again shorter. When she slid the page over, Sam saw that she had written *the grey corridor* and then, in the margin, in a different pressure, *the corridor*. Then the first version again, restored, with a small tick beside it.

Lee read it standing. "Grey," he said.

"It's how she'd see it."
