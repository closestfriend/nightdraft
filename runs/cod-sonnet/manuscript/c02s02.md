Ben's station went red a little after the meal break. It pulsed in the corner of the wall monitor beside his account name, and Lee read the name aloud, not loudly, the way a person reads a number off a ticket at a pharmacy.

Ben finished his sentence first. Sam watched him do it. He took the sunflower-seed cup from beside his keyboard, folded the top edge over so the shells would not spill, and set it in the bin. Then he removed his headset, wound the cord around two fingers, and hung it on the hook, because headsets were counted at the end of shift and a headset on a hook was one less thing to explain. Two men Sam had not seen before were standing at the door of the next room. They were polite about it. One held the door.

The chat on Ben's screen was still open. A customer named Dale, or someone using Dale, had written *are u there baby* and the response window had turned amber. A woman from the far row was sent over to sit in Ben's warm chair. She adjusted the height, read forty seconds of archive, and typed, *sorry, I dropped off, my phone died*. Sam recognized the lie because he had used it himself on Tuesday.

Then the sound started. It was not loud. It was a wall, and the sound was what a wall does with it: a flat, patient, regular sound with pauses in it where somebody was getting his breath or somebody was resetting. Nobody looked up. The woman two seats over typed faster, and then the room found it, all thirty keyboards, until the typing was a rain on a roof and the roof had a drum under it. Sam realized he was typing *and honestly, I think* in the same time. He stopped. His hands hovered over the keys with the cursor blinking after *think*. Then, because a hovering hand shows on the activity graph, he finished the sentence with something about tea.

When it was over, Ben was walked back to his seat. His chair was occupied, so the woman gave it up and he sat in it. He put the headset back on. He did not have the cup, so he did nothing with his hands.

Lee updated the wall monitor. Under Ben's name a yellow band appeared and the word PIP. A moment later, under Sam's, in the same yellow, the same word, and after it a column Sam had never noticed had a header: REVIEW. A date was in it. It was a Friday, eleven days away. Somebody had known that date before this morning. It had been sitting in the system beside his name the whole time, like the fee in a contract, and the only thing that had changed was that it was now displayed.

So he wrote to Paul at length.

Paul had said, two weeks back, that a water main was a record of every decision anyone had made about a town, and Sam took that and built on it for six hundred words. He wrote that a pipe replaced section by section was the same pipe only in the ledger, and that maybe a person was that too, a ledger with a continuous name. He disagreed with Paul about lead service lines, cautiously, in a way that gave Paul room to be right. Ana would have been proud of the *hm,*. He put it in twice. The message's dwell timer, when Paul's answer came, would be long, which meant Paul would sit with it, which meant the account would be alive at the end of the week. Sam did not phrase it to himself that way. He told himself he was writing well.

Near the end, Paul had mentioned that his father had run the same line of work and had never once discussed it at dinner. Sam wrote: *I think I've been afraid of that too, of being the kind of person who is only ever at work.*

The flag arrived before the send confirmation.

`QA / ACCT: GRACE_LIN / OP: SAM / SEV 2`
`TAG: EMOTIONALLY RECIPROCAL. Persona discloses fear in response to target disclosure.`
`Guideline 4.3: Grace receives. Grace does not match. Recommend Variant A. Cumulative: 2.`

Lee had not even turned his head. Sam retrieved the message from the queue. He deleted the sentence one letter at a time from the end, because select-and-delete left a mark in the log, and when it was gone the paragraph closed over the place where it had been without a seam. The message was better without it, and he could see that it was. He put *ok?* at the bottom, lower case, one question mark, the way she had.
