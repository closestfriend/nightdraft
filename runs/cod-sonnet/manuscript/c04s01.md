Paul wrote to Camila Reis the way he wrote to residents about a boil-water advisory: what he knew, how he knew it, what he did not know, whom to contact. It took four drafts. The first one began *Dear Ms. Reis* and the last one began *Hello*, because he'd decided that formality would read as an accusation. He gave the name of the account, the six months, and the two photographs he'd matched, the one on the balcony and the one with the paper cup. He wrote: *I don't want anything from you. I thought you would want to know.* He sent it through the professional page of the dental practice in Niterói, under a photograph of her in a blue mask, and then he went and re-seated a flange gasket that did not need re-seating.

She answered before he had finished lunch. Her English was quick and had no punctuation to spare. She had known for two years. Eleven men so far had written to her with a version of his message, each one kind, each one wanting her to see that *he* was the one who'd noticed. Did he want a medal? Did he want her to say he was not like the others? Then two lines in Portuguese he ran through the translation tool, which returned them as *Go and be sorry in your own house.*

He answered that she was right and that he was sorry. That was the last thing he sent.

By evening a friend of his, Dennis Okafor, who ran the pump station on the east side and whose mother had lost a condominium to a man in Lagos, sent a link with no comment. It went to a forum called Faces Not Names. Camila had used the forum's report form. It had fields: NAME OF CONTACT, PLATFORM, AMOUNT LOST, STILL IN CONTACT (Y/N). She had filled in *Paul Whitaker, Ohio*, and under AMOUNT LOST she had typed *0*, and under STILL IN CONTACT she had typed *Y, he says*. She had attached the screenshots. They were of his message to her, and also of the thread he had pasted into it to prove the match, forty lines of his own paragraphs to Grace. There was the one where he had written that her letter about the ferry was the best prose anyone had sent him since a college roommate died.

There were sixty-one comments. A moderator had tagged him *Reported Contact*. One user wrote *$0 lol, give it time.* Another wrote *the ones who think they're too smart are the whole business model.* A third, with a small avatar of a teakettle, wrote *sir you are an idiot,* and eleven people had pressed the button beside it.

Nothing in it was false. That was what he kept returning to, sitting at the kitchen table with his boots still on. The message was his, and so was the ferry line. He had pasted it himself. Camila Reis had done what a person does with a stranger's evidence, and she owed him nothing, not an answer or a paragraph of it. He tried the sentence *she is entitled to this* several times and it held up each time, and it did not help.

Dennis called and he let it ring out. Then Dennis texted: *Not to lecture.* Then: *You want to come over.* Then, four minutes later: *Paul, it's the one from the letters, isn't it.*

He did not answer that either. He took the screenshots off his phone, one at a time, and the phone asked him each time whether he was sure.

The compound ran eleven hours ahead of him. He'd worked it out in the second month from her timestamps and kept the figure on a sticky note inside a drawer, like a pipe diameter. He knew, therefore, what hour it was for her when he opened the thread. He knew the hour and typed anyway, with two fingers, slowly, deleting a *Dear Grace* and a long paragraph that began *You should know that today*. What was left was short.

*I was told I'm an idiot today. Are you still there?*

He pressed send and watched the word *Delivered* appear under it, gray, and then not change.
