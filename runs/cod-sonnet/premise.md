# Premise

**Engine.** A trafficked worker in a scam compound on the Myanmar border is handed an established romantic persona — a woman, six months into a relationship with an American man — and ordered to convert the affair into deposits. The American worked out almost immediately that the photographs are stolen. He stayed anyway, because whoever is behind the account fascinates him. Now a scammer is running a con on a man who knows he is being conned, and each side keeps up the fiction because neither can bear for the conversation to end.

**Distributed authorship.** "She" is a corporate account, not a person. Shifts hand her between workers; a guidelines document and an archive of past messages keep her consistent; tiny quirks in her language betray which human is typing. The American's most intimate relationship is with a composite. The new operator, reading the archive, grows jealous of the earlier operator who wrote the formative months — and eventually of himself-as-the-persona, because the American knows that constructed version of him better than anyone knows the real him.

**The two men, as contradictions rather than biographies.**
- The American: almost impossible to scam for money, embarrassingly susceptible to intellectual intimacy. Intelligent, online, suspicious, lonely. His intelligence does not protect him from humiliation.
- The operator: excellent at manipulating strangers, constitutionally unable to lie convincingly about himself. Conscripted into this labor; skilled at it in ways that shame him.
Names are not fixed here — give them plain, unremarkable names. Do not name real organizations, militias, or people; keep the geography impressionistic.

**The machine (keep these rules consistent).** Supervisors assign accounts. Conversations are handed between shifts. A persona guidelines document dictates her style (she uses commas and heart emojis, never semicolons; QA flags violations). A dashboard raises CONVERSION OVERDUE alerts. Workers are scored on conversion-to-deposit ratio and put on performance improvement plans — including for discouraging a target from a larger deposit, or for a persona becoming "too emotionally reciprocal." Supervisors A/B test vulnerability. Translation software mediates. Missing quota is punished by beatings in the next room while the office keeps working. The comedy is procedural and the horror is that the procedure is real. Never wink at it.

**Scene spine — about twelve irreversible events, in order.**
1. The operator starts a shift and is assigned an existing account. He must read six months of someone else's love affair before he is allowed to be her. In the middle of a horrifying place his first reaction is that the previous operator completely misunderstood this man.
2. He studies the archive and the guidelines and finds the earlier operator's small tells.
3. His first unscripted exchange: he says something true in her voice. The American notices, and is delighted.
4. The American deposits nothing. The supervisor notices the ratio.
5. The American realizes the photographs are stolen. He does not confront; he asks a question instead. Both keep going.
6. The previous operator returns.
7. (Planner: invent the next three or four irreversible events — each must make the situation worse and change the relationship, not "they talk some more." One should be a 3 AM conversation about something like Kierkegaard while the dashboard screams CONVERSION OVERDUE.)
8. The compound collapses.
9. Ending, the gravitational fact the whole book leans toward: the American finally meets the operator who loved him — and discovers that the messages he loved most were written by somebody else.

**What the book is about (never state it in the prose).** Distributed authenticity. Was the intimacy fake if the feelings became real? Who exactly did he fall in love with — the photographs, the character bible, the first operator, or the man who inherited the account? This is not a story about a scammer developing a conscience. No redemption arc. No moralizing. The reader is never told how to classify anyone.

**Outline constraints.** Every beat is a concrete event: who does what, with which object or system, and what irreversibly changes. No beat may be a mood ("deepening conflict", "they part without resolution"). Mark never wires any money at any point. Mark must not learn that the messages he loved most were written by another operator until the final scene. Include the 3 AM conversation about Kierkegaard as its own beat, with the dashboard alerting throughout. The two men's intelligence must fail them in specific, humiliating ways.
