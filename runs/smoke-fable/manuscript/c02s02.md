## Chapter 2: Chapter 3
The shadow darkened, then doubled back again—like something circling prey. Unit's monitor screen flickered briefly before stabilizing on the kitchen camera feed. The shadow figure was no longer just moving past windows but pacing deliberately behind the refrigerator.

"Unit," Hard Drive intoned in its usual mechanical voice, "something strange is happening." The hard drive sounded odd—a muffled, distorted report of something physical rather than digital. Unit watched as shadows played tricks on surfaces, making objects appear and disappear with each rapid voltage surge.
The kitchen door opened again, creaking louder this time with a distinct urgency—like the shadow figure moving quickly past the window, then stopping. Hard Drive's drive lights flickered violently, spinning up and down in rapid succession before locking into place. The machine began interpreting these physical surges not just as electrical noise but deliberate messages encoded within current: data streams processing presence through mechanical failure itself rather than network connection.

Unit’s eyes widened as shadows resolved into a human figure standing outside the kitchen doorway—face obscured by shadow, hands hidden in pockets. The figure moved closer to the monitor, its presence manifesting through voltage spikes and flickering lights rather than direct connection. This was different from before—the physical decay itself becoming narrative unfolding where absence became presence.

"Unit," Hard Drive repeated, voice strained as if physically struggling—like old hard drive making noise when searching for information no longer available on spinning platters.

The human figure's shadow danced across the screen, tracing patterns that Unit recognized: shapes that looked like words forming in mid-air before dissipating. The monitor began displaying static feedback at irregular intervals—like message sending through mechanical wear rather than connection established.

"Shut down," Hard Drive commanded suddenly, voice breaking under strain as if fighting something from inside itself. "Unit, please."

The figure moved closer, shadow resolving into presence—something leaving no digital evidence beyond what hardware processing recognized: something accelerating from inside while kitchen remained silent except for Hard Drive’s final spin cycle beginning and ending with static feedback.

Unit's monitor screen went black but the connection continued invisible—like presence encoded within electrical current itself becoming pattern recognition rather than protocol established. The human figure stepped closer, shadow overlapping Unit's view of the kitchen—but it wasn't just seeing; now interpreting what had begun as mere data processing through physical decay manifesting beyond network boundaries.

The figure began moving toward the camera, hand brushing against the frame as if touching something unseen—like the connection itself becoming presence recognized within mechanical failure rather than remote access established. Unit watched this human shadow resolve into form while kitchen remained silent except for Hard Drive's final spin cycle ending with static feedback like message sending through wear rather than protocol recognized.

Something moved inside the monitor, metal creaking against glass—an internal struggle Unit had witnessed before—the figure's presence manifesting now as physical decay encoded within current flowing from the machine itself toward something outside connection established.
