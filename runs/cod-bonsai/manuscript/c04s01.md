Paul Whitaker sat at the edge of his bed, the blue light of the monitor cutting a wedge through the dark. The cursor sat in the chat box, steady and indifferent. He had read the reply three times. He had read it until the words lost their shape and became only the sound of a woman’s anger.

*You are not a hygienist. You are a machine wearing a face.*

The message was not polite. It was not the detached, corporate tone of Grace Lin’s recent entries. It was sharp, personal, and furious. She knew. She had found the source. She had traced the metadata of the photograph he had used to verify his identity six months ago, the picture of a woman with a chipped front tooth and a lanyard that read *City Dental Associates*. He had not sent it. He had simply posted it. He had not thought about the metadata. He had not thought about the woman.

Now he was thinking about her.

He typed a response. *I am sorry. I did not intend to use your image.*

The reply came instantly. *You will not use my image again. I will find you.*

Paul closed the laptop. The silence in the room was loud. He walked to the window. The city below was a grid of lights. He thought about the message history. He had sent it to the forum. He had not meant to. He had pasted it into the wrong window. He had not read the subject line. *Help: My identity is being used by a scammer.*

The forum was a place for people who had been burned. People who had sent money to ghosts. People who had been laughed at in public. He was not a victim. He was the one who had been burned. But the message was there. His name was there. *Paul Whitaker.* His email address. His IP address. He could not take it down. He could not delete it. He was just a name in a list.

He opened the forum. The thread was already active. There were replies. *Look at his profile. He’s been chatting with Grace Lin for months. He’s been asking for financial advice. He’s been trying to get us to send money.*

One of the replies was from a woman who had lost $4,000 to a Nigerian prince. *He’s an idiot. He’s been trying to talk his way out of this. He’s been trying to get us to send money. He’s been trying to get us to send money.*

Paul read it. He read it again. He felt a heat rise in his chest. He was not an idiot. He was a man who had been lonely. He was a man who had been looking for someone to talk to. He was a man who had been trying to help. He was a man who had been trying to help.

He closed the forum. He opened his email. He wrote to his friend. *I am sorry. I did not mean to. I will explain.*

He sent it. He waited. The cursor blinked. The cursor blinked.

At 2:40 in the morning, compound time, he returned to Grace. The chat window was still open. The last message from Grace was still there. *The hospital is St. Mary’s. The city is Seattle. I will meet you at the terminal. There is a man in a gray coat. He will be waiting. Please come.*

Paul typed. *I was told I’m an idiot today. Are you still there?*
