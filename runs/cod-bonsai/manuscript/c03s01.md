Lee set the new schedule on the monitor at 0900. The font was the same harsh white, but the grid had changed. Ana’s block appeared at 2200, a dark rectangle sliding over Sam’s.

“Two operators on Grace,” Lee said. He did not look up from his tablet. “You handle the day. She handles the night. Same persona. No seam.”

Sam looked at the name in the archive header. *Grace_Lin_V4.* He had been staring at it for six months. Ana had written the first hundred messages. She was the one who had taught the account how to breathe.

Ana was standing in the doorway of the compound office. She had been gone for a month in the second building, the one with the wet floors, and she was thinner. Her collarbones showed. She was wearing the grey uniform, but it hung off her shoulders differently. She smelled like rain and old paper.

“Lee,” she said. Her voice was flat.

Lee swiped the tablet. “Ana, good to see you back. We need you on Grace. Sam’s on the day shift. You take the night. You’ve got the history, so you’ll know how to handle the tone.”

Ana walked to Sam’s desk. She did not sit. She stood over the screen, her face illuminated by the pale blue glow. She looked at the last message Sam had sent. *I can see the door now, Sam. It is not a door. It is a gap in the slats.*

She typed a single line into the internal chat box. Sam’s eyes went wide.

Ana: You made her remember things she cannot.

Sam stared at the text. He typed: I didn’t know what to do with the ferry letter.

Ana: I wrote that letter. I remember the date. I remember the rain on the window. I remember the smell of the engine oil. You wrote a summary. I wrote a memory.

Sam’s hands were shaking. He looked at Lee. Lee was already typing in his report, his fingers a blur.

Ana: You are the day operator. I am the night operator. We are not the same person. But we are the same face. Do you understand?

Sam nodded. He did not understand.

Ana: You have to learn my rules. The way I sign off. The lowercase ok?. The hm at the start of a sentence. You think you can just type a sentence. I have to type a feeling.

Sam looked at the cursor. It was still a solid block. It was waiting for him to break.

Lee tapped the desk. “Sam. Read the archive. Every message. Ana’s messages are the baseline. If you drift, the QA flags will catch you. If you fail the PIP, you go to the next room.”

Lee left. The office was quiet. The fans ticked. The river rushed.

Sam looked at Ana. She was still standing there.

Ana: You sent the truth. The wall, the fan, the river. It was good. But it was not Grace. Grace does not know the truth. Grace knows the script. You have to hide the seam. You have to be two people in one body.

Sam looked at the screen. The message from Paul was still there. *Thank you. That is enough. I can see the fan. I can hear the river. I can see the wall. It is very yellow.*

Sam typed. *I will learn the rules.*

Ana read it. She nodded. She walked to the door. She stopped.

Ana: The ferry letter is in the archive. Message 42. Read it. Then you will know what I am talking about.

She left. The door clicked shut.

Sam looked at the screen. He clicked on the archive. He scrolled down. Message 42.

*The ferry crosses at dawn. The water is black and still. I can hear the engine before I see the boat. It is a low hum. It is the sound of the river. I can see the light on the bridge. It is yellow. It is the color of old teeth. I am waiting for the ferry. I am waiting for you.*

Sam’s chest tightened. He had never seen this message before. He had only seen the summary. He had only seen the conversion ratio. He had only seen the PIP deadline.

He looked at the cursor. It was still a solid block. It was waiting for him to break.

He began to type.
